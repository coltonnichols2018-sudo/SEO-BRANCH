Most people keep their mattress way too long. The average mattress should be replaced every 7-10 years, but many people hold on for 12, 15, even 20 years. If you're not sure whether it's time for a new one, here are five clear signs your mattress is telling you to move on.

1. You Wake Up Sore or Stiff

This is the #1 sign. If you wake up every morning with back pain, neck pain, or general stiffness that fades after you've been up for 30 minutes, your mattress is the likely culprit. A good mattress should make you feel better in the morning, not worse.

Over time, the foam and springs in your mattress break down and stop supporting your body properly. Your spine falls out of alignment, your muscles work overtime to compensate, and you wake up feeling like you didn't sleep at all.

2. You Can See or Feel Sagging

Run your hand across your mattress. Do you feel dips, lumps, or valleys — especially where you normally sleep? Can you see a visible body impression when you stand back and look at it?

Sagging means the support layers have permanently compressed. No amount of flipping or rotating will fix it. Once a mattress sags, it's done.

3. You Sleep Better Somewhere Else

Pay attention to how you sleep on vacation, at a friend's house, or even on the couch. If you consistently sleep better somewhere other than your own bed, your mattress is the problem — not your sleep habits.

4. Your Allergies Are Getting Worse

Over the years, mattresses accumulate dust mites, dead skin cells, and allergens deep in the fabric and foam. If you've noticed increasing allergy symptoms — sneezing, congestion, itchy eyes — especially when you first lie down or wake up, your mattress may be harboring more than just memories.

Even with mattress protectors and regular cleaning, an old mattress eventually becomes an allergen factory.

5. It's Simply Old

If you can't remember when you bought your mattress — or if you can, and it was during the Obama administration — it's time. Even if your mattress still "feels okay," the materials have degraded significantly after 8+ years.

What to Do Next
